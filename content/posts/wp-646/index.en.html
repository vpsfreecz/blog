A look back at our first day hosting the video-calling platform. Word spread quickly, and we had hundreds of calls at once.

A few days ago, we agreed that the current situation called for as many ideas and helping hands as possible, to come up with something useful. Even a small improvement counts.

On Monday evening, the idea of a public videoconferencing platform came up. Overnight into Tuesday, we launched a public instance of Jitsi Meet. Word spread quickly, and people began arranging meetings, calling for a chat and planning other uses. To join them, visit meet.vpsfree.cz.

Articles on several sites helped spread the word: živě.cz, ABCLinuxu.cz, Lupa.cz, iDnes.cz and surely others. There was also a big response on social media, where groups including teachers shared the link.

The first day was hectic, with many people asking for help with various problems, hosting small services and similar things. Our friends at Master datacentre, where our infrastructure lives, also contacted us. They offered a free increase in link capacity so we could handle more calls. They doubled our connectivity to the Czech internet and quadrupled it to Slovakia. Thank you very much.

The number of simultaneous calls grew quickly during the first day. We recorded 330 people calling at once, and that will probably keep growing. At peaks, the server transfers around 100 Mbps of traffic, with CPU usage currently about half capacity. There was a service outage overnight, but traffic was minimal then.

Interestingly, users abroad discovered the service too: we had calls from China and other parts of the world. This hasn't caused problems or heavy load, so we're leaving it as it is. If it starts causing problems for Czech users, we'd have to introduce some restrictions on international calls.

Keep your fingers crossed for us, support us, and we'll get through this together.
